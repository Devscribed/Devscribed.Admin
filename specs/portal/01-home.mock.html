portal/01 — Home

The screen a signed-in member lands on. Two halves: what is mine on the left, what the organization did on the right. Drawn from the design system's own components — Card variant="panel" (§66), Avatar (§93), Badge (§32, §59), EmptyState (§28, §65), Preloader (§23) — and from tokens only.

What the mock is asking you to look at

  • The heading. Drawn here as a greeting with the person's name. The alternative is a heading that names the page (Home, or the organization) and puts the name nowhere — a portal greets, a report does not.
  • Which half leads. Personal on the left, feed on the right at 400px. The feed column is bounded rather than stretched because an entry is a sentence, and a sentence has a width at which it reads (§84's argument, on a feed).
  • Days, never money. A user reading their own vacation is answered days and a null balance (apps/api/src/vacation/vacation.service.ts:108). Drawing money here would make this screen role-dependent for no product reason.
  • The empty feed. State 2 is the day the feature ships into an organization that has just been created. State 3 is the same screen after an admin has switched two of the three groups off.
  • Whether an entry is a card at all. Each is drawn as its own panel. The alternative is one panel holding a divided list, which is quieter and reads faster but leaves nothing for spec 03 to hang a reaction on.
  • Every entry opens. States 6–8 are the page behind one. It is the portal's own page rather than a jump to the source record, because the source records are gated: manage-projects and canManageHiring are admin and manager, so a link to a project or a vacancy would be a dead end for the role the feed is mostly read by. Where the reader may open the record, the page offers it as a link.
  • What a trimmed page does not say. State 8 draws less than state 7 and says nothing about it. A page that named what it was withholding would be telling a reader about a door they cannot open.
